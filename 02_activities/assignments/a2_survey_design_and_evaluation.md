Part A - Survey Design: Political Strategy & Voter Sentiment
Scenario Selection: #2 (Canadian National Political Party)

1. Purpose of the Survey
The primary purpose of this survey is to identify the specific policy priorities and leadership traits that will swing "undecided" voters in key battleground ridings. By understanding the gap between current approval ratings and actual "intent to vote," the party can refine its messaging in the final 30 days of the campaign to secure a majority mandate.

2. Sampling Design
Target Population: All Canadian citizens aged 18 or older who are eligible to vote in the upcoming federal election.

Sampling Frame: The National Register of Electors, supplemented by commercial geographic databases to ensure coverage of newly eligible voters and those who have recently moved.

Sampling Units: Individual households within targeted electoral districts (ridings).

Observational Units: One randomly selected eligible voter per household.

Overall Sampling Strategy: A Disproportional Stratified Random Sampling approach will be used.

The population will be stratified by Province and Riding Type (Safe vs. Battleground).

"Battleground" ridings (where the margin of victory was <5% in the last election) will be oversampled to provide higher statistical power for localized messaging.

Weighting will be applied post-collection to ensure the final results reflect the actual national demographic distribution.

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type: A cross-sectional probability sample. It uses a stratified design where one person is randomly selected per household.
2. Sample size: Approximately 50,000 households were initially sampled, resulting in roughly 24,000 completed responses.
3. Target population: Non-institutionalized persons aged 15 years and older living in the ten Canadian provinces. Excludes territories and full-time residents of institutions.
4. Sampling frame: The sampling frame combines landline and cellular telephone numbers from Census and administrative sources with Statistics Canada’s dwelling frame. Records consist of groups of telephone numbers associated with the same address, or a single telephone number when an address cannot be linked. This frame improves coverage of households with telephone access.
5. Survey mode(s): Mixed-mode: Electronic Questionnaire (EQ) (self-administered online) and Computer-Assisted Telephone Interviewing (CATI).
6. Timeline: Data collection occurred from September 4, 2018, to December 28, 2018.
7. Response rate: The overall response rate was 41.9%.
8. Weights: Person-level weights (WGHT_PER) are used to represent the population. Bootstrap weights are provided for calculating variance and sampling error.
9. Data processing:Conducted via the Social Survey Processing Environment (SSPE). Includes coding of open-ended responses (e.g., industry/occupation) into standard classifications.
10. Cleaning, imputation, etc: Used donor imputation (nearest-neighbor) for missing values. Crucially, income data was largely derived through linkage to tax records (T1FF) to reduce respondent burden and error.
11. Sources of error: Includes Sampling error (measured by CV and bootstrap) and Non-sampling errors such as coverage bias (excluding those without phones) and non-response bias.
12. Limitations, known biases, etc: Non-response bias is a significant factor due to the ~42% response rate. Social Desirability Bias is also common in this specific survey, as respondents may over-report "charitable giving" or "volunteering."
13. Link to documentation and any additional sources used
```
